## 1 · THE FINDING THAT SHOULD BE READ FIRST

**The AI-provider prohibition may already be in force, and may have been since January.**

§4.7 of the **2026-09-23** Meta Terms is what B4·2 Part 2 read, and Part 2 treated it as **new
in fifteen days**. Searching for the Business Agents Terms today surfaced contemporaneous
reporting of a distinct, earlier event: **Meta's WhatsApp Business API barring general-purpose AI
chatbots from 15 January 2026**, via updated Business API terms, with the reported carve-out that
*AI tools cannot be the primary functionality offered via the Business API, though AI integrated
as part of broader business services — like customer-support bots — will still be permitted.*

**That is §4.7(a)'s test, in the same words, with a date eight months in the past.**

**NOT DERIVED, and this is the point.** These are news reports, not Meta's own text. This seat
has **not** located the January change in any Meta document it has read. Three readings are
possible and they matter differently:

1. **The January change lives in a document not yet read** — most likely the **WhatsApp Business
   Messaging Policy** or the **Solution Provider terms**, both on F-41.33's unread list. Then the
   prohibition has bound TDW since January and §4.7 merely codifies it into the Meta Terms.
2. **The January change *is* §4.7**, pre-announced in January and taking effect 09-23. Then
   Part 2's reading stands unamended.
3. **The reporting is inaccurate.**

**Reading 1 is the one that costs something**, and it is cheap to settle: the Messaging Policy is
public and fetchable. **It should be the next document read, ahead of the Business Agents
Terms**, because it may move a compliance date eight months earlier — and R-41.64's position was
built on the assumption the clause is prospective.

**What does not change on any reading:** the estate's position under R-41.64 is the carve-out the
reporting describes. *AI integrated as part of broader business services* is precisely what TDW
is building to be. The reporting, if accurate, is **evidence that the carve-out is real and
survives contact with enforcement** — Meta named it in the same breath as the ban.

access. **A founder paste is owed.**

- **Meta may suppress TDW's own web chat.** *"To operate Business Agents, you authorise Meta to:
  disable and/or suppress your website's pop-ups or web-chat in the interfaces where Business
  Agents are deployed."* The bride and vendor PWAs both carry chat surfaces.

  rights and a lawful basis to grant Meta the rights under those terms.

**Why this matters even though TDW uses no Business Agents today:** the Tech Provider Terms are
titled *Meta Business Messaging **and Meta Business Agent** Technology Provider Terms* and
incorporate the Business Agents Terms by reference. **Clicking Accept binds TDW to a product it
does not use and to authorisations over surfaces it does own.** That is the sharpest single
argument for R-41.57 — the founder decides, on a complete read.
