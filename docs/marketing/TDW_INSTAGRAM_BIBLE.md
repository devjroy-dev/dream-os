## 0 · WHAT THIS PAGE IS

Not a product page. Not a founder's hustle diary. Not a trends account.

It is a **raw, unscripted record of two people building a company for the Indian wedding industry from inside it** — Swati, a working makeup artist who belongs to that industry, and Dev, the outsider who married into it and is building the thing it needs. Around them: the vendors they visit, the vendors who come for coffee, the brides they call, the friends who argue at their table, and the interns who are hired for the nerve to say "no, that's wrong."

The product exists in the background. It is never the subject. Everyone watching knows this is the team building The Dream Wedding; nobody is ever sold it. **You market the product by attracting people to a story, and the story is the industry itself.**

The one-line promise to a vendor who finds the page: *these people understand my business better than I've ever heard it described — and one of them does my job.*

---

## 1 · THE RULES (the whole format in nine lines)

1. **Nothing is scripted.** Rooms are agreed before the camera rolls (what the argument is about); endings are not.
2. **Nobody on this page is ever asked to say something they don't think.** Swati, guests, interns, brides, Dev. This is the rule that makes vendors trust the company behind the page.
3. **She is the expert. He is the translator.** Her authority is craft and the industry; his is scale and systems. The argument between the two is the engine. When she is right, he says so on camera.
4. **The product is never the subject.** It may be the answer, the thing on the phone that buzzes, the calendar the coffee got booked into. It is never the topic.
5. **Consent is law, not courtesy.** A face, a name, a number, a client's story — nothing goes on camera without the person's explicit yes. If they say no, the episode becomes the two of you retelling it afterwards, unnamed. (§6.)
6. **Let yourself lose.** The most shared episodes are the ones where the artist wins and the tech guy changes the plan.
7. **Accuracy is the price of being on camera with Swati.** Anything off by a degree, she catches. Come with the number, the source, the reason — 500% right, not 100%.
8. **No lecturing.** If an episode could be titled "How to…", it belongs on LinkedIn, not here. The audience learns by watching people who are figuring it out.
9. **Measure vendors, not views.** (§7.)

## 4 · THE GROWTH MECHANISM (why this page grows, in order of force)

1. **Featuring vendors is what gets vendors to share.** Nobody reposts a feature demo; everyone reposts a clip they're in. Every coffee, visit and day-with credits the vendor by name (with consent) and tags every vendor on that wedding — photographer, MUA, décor, venue. This is the **wedding credits** feature of Block 19 running as content before it is built. Sharing is the whole game.
2. **The couple who is the customer.** Vendors follow a working makeup artist; founders follow founders. Swati on camera is the reason the right audience arrives.
3. **The argument.** Scale vs craft is a genre; people take sides and come back.
4. **The on-ramps.** Table-of-four debates and season BTS are what strangers find; the kitchen table is what they stay for. A little of the first feeds the second — never let the on-ramp become the page.
5. **The concierge call is proof.** Every F4 is the product happening in public without being named.
6. **Dogfooding.** The Sunday numbers, the open-date stories, the reel-from-a-gallery — the page becomes the reference account for TDW's own Instagram features. "This is how we post" is shown with your own numbers.
7. **Series hooks.** "Coffee with the vendor who…" · "A day with…" · "The bride who wanted…" — recurring titles the algorithm and the audience both recognise.
8. **Collaborators' audiences.** Every guest brings theirs; the collab post is shared to both. Stylists, venues and influencers on the shoot board (Block 19 G5) are the natural guest list.
9. **Cross-posting with intent.** Reels to YouTube Shorts; the longer cuts to YouTube; the founder-shaped moments (F6, F7) to LinkedIn where founders live. The Instagram page stays for the industry.

## 5 · CADENCE AND PRODUCTION

**Off-season (now → October):** 3 posts/week — one kitchen table, one coffee or visit, one table-of-four or debrief. Batch the visits: one afternoon, three stops. Sunday numbers every week.
**Season (November → February):** the documentarian is hired; one day-with per week becomes the spine; kitchen tables continue at whatever survives. **If only one thing can be sustained, it is the kitchen table.** One good episode a week beats three in month one and none in November.

**Gear:** a phone, a lav mic each, the garden's light. Nothing that makes it look produced.
**Language:** whatever is spoken at the table — Hindi-English as it comes. Captions always (most watch muted).
**Editing rule:** cut for the moment, not the message. The take where she laughs at his idea stays.
**Titles:** the argument, in her words if possible ("Why does everyone need to be on Google?").
**Thumbnails/covers:** faces mid-argument, not the logo.
**Hashtags/tags:** every vendor on camera, every venue, every product mentioned that agreed to be.
**Music:** none in dialogue; original audio is an asset.
**Story cadence:** Sunday numbers; behind-the-day snippets; open dates when the feature ships.

**The bible's operating line:** run the show on the product. Coffees and visits on TDW's calendar; guests logged as leads; concierge requests taken through the real notification. Not for the camera — because every intern scheduling an episode is a cold walk that finds what's clumsy before a paying vendor does.

## 6 · CONSENT AND PRIVACY LAW

- **Explicit yes, every time**, before anyone's face, name, business, phone number, client story or product opinion goes on camera. A one-line written consent (WhatsApp message counts) kept with the footage.
- **Brides:** the call is voice-only by default; her name and her wedding's details are never on camera without her yes; if no, the episode is the debrief (F6), unnamed.
- **Vendors' clients:** a vendor may talk about "a client who…" but never name one. If a story identifies someone, it is retold without the identifying detail or cut.
- **Third parties in the frame:** guests at a day-with wedding are background only; no face is held; the couple of that wedding consents to the day-with in advance, in writing.
- **Products and brands:** honest opinions are fine (why YSL); nothing is presented as sponsored unless it is, and if it ever is, it is said on camera.
- **Money:** a vendor's prices, another vendor's prices, a bride's budget — only with the owner's yes, and never a number that lets a viewer identify a specific booking.
- **The withdrawal right:** anyone can ask for their episode to come down, and it comes down.
- **Off-camera is off-camera.** Whatever a vendor says with the phone in the pocket stays there unless they say otherwise.

---

## 7 · WHAT TO MEASURE

Not views. Two numbers, weekly:
1. **Vendors who DM the page asking how to get on.**
2. **Vendors who share a clip they're in.**
If both rise, the page is working at three thousand followers. If views rise and these don't, the wrong people are being entertained.

Secondary: saves on kitchen-table episodes (the argument is being kept), comments that take a side, concierge requests that arrive from the page.
