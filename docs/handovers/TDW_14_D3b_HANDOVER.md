## 3 · THE GAP THE VETO SURFACED — a payload change to a sealed door

**Frozen byte ⑤ reads `{n} of {total} voted`, and its denominator is THE CIRCLE** — how many people could answer, against how many did. **D-3a's payload did not carry it**, because nothing had asked. No client can derive it: the member's session tells her about herself, and the bride's bloom would have to count a roster the payload never mentions; either surface computing it would also be a second implementation of one number.

So `polls.js` serves `eligible_count` = active members **+ the bride**. `circle_members` holds members only, and R-D3.2's whole point is that she is a participant like any other — **a denominator excluding her would say "2 of 2 voted" while three people held a vote** (§10.1). A circle with no members still counts one, never zero (§10.2). The count asks for a number and no rows (§10.4), and is taken **once per page, never per poll** (§10.5).

**This is a change to a door D-3a sealed, and it is the chair's to reverse.** The veto ratified a byte whose data did not exist; this is the smallest honest way to make the byte true.
